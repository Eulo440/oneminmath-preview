Mad minute math, online and free

A mad minute is the timed math drill many of us remember from school: a sheet of facts, a one-minute timer, and a quiet count of how many you got right. It builds fluency, the ability to recall facts like 7 × 8 or 13 − 6 instantly, so working memory is free for the harder parts of math.

One Minute Math (this site used to be called Mad Minute Math) is that same drill on a screen. You get one problem at a time, answers check themselves as you type, and after 60 seconds you see your score, the facts you missed, and how you compare with your own best.

How a round works

  1. Pick an operation (add, subtract, multiply, divide or mixed), a level, and whole numbers, fractions or decimals.
  2. Press Start. A short countdown, then the clock runs for one minute.
  3. Type each answer. There is no Enter key to press; the next problem appears as soon as you are right.
  4. At the end you see your score, your misses with the right answers, and a one-tap round of just those misses.

Tips for a good mad minute habit

  • Short and often beats long and rare. Two or three one-minute rounds a day do more than a long weekly session.
  • Compete with yourself. Your progress chart shows your last rounds and your best. Beating last week is the goal, not beating a classmate.
  • Fix the misses right away. Use Redo my misses after a round, or the Missed questions clock option, which keeps a fact until you get it right twice in a row.
  • Move up when it feels easy. When a level gives steady scores without misses, try the next one.
