Decimal practice

Decimals show up everywhere: money, measurements, sports stats. One Minute Math drills the core skills with exact answers, so 0.1 + 0.2 really is 0.3. Type answers with a dot (2.5 or .5); a trailing zero like 0.50 is fine too.

Start adding decimalsAll four, no clock

What each level covers

  • Adding: tenths (2.4 + 3.8), hundredths (3.45 + 1.27), then mixed places (4 + 0.37).
  • Subtracting: tenths, hundredths, then mixed places like 5 − 1.25.
  • Multiplying: tenths × whole numbers and × 10 or × 100, then tenths × tenths, then decimal × decimal (1.5 × 0.3).
  • Dividing: ÷ 10 and ÷ 100, then ÷ 0.1, 0.2 and 0.5, then decimal ÷ decimal (1.2 ÷ 0.3).

Quick reminders

  • Line up the decimal points to add or subtract: 6.42 − 2.15 = 4.27.
  • To multiply, ignore the dots, multiply, then count the decimal places: 1.5 × 0.3 → 15 × 3 = 45 → 0.45.
  • Dividing by 0.5 is the same as doubling: 3.5 ÷ 0.5 = 7.

Also try fraction practice. Seeing that 0.5 = 1/2 and 0.25 = 1/4 helps with both.
